Remove bad sensor data

A heart-rate strap with a dying battery. A cadence magnet that slipped. A power meter that showed 2000 watts for six seconds. Find the spikes on the chart, then remove them or replace them with a value you choose.

Averages and maximums change with your edit, on every lap and for the whole session.

Fill the gap from a friend's file

Your head unit died halfway through the ride, but a friend recorded it too. Combine their file with yours and choose what to take, one data track at a time: GPS, heart rate, power, cadence, speed or temperature.

If the two clocks disagree, shift the second file until the recordings line up.

Read the guide

Cut out the stop

You sat in a café and your watch kept going. Your bike computer counted the walk to the restaurant as a very slow ride. Mark that section with the handles on the chart and cut it out.

The stats change with it. The same goes for ten minutes of waiting at the trailhead.

Correct what the file says

Set the right sport. Change the device the file names. Set the total elevation gain when the altimeter drifted.

Remove private data before you share

Remove GPS coordinates, the device serial number, or your athlete profile. Anonymize takes all three out in one step.

See every edit first

You see what an edit changes before you apply it. Undo takes back the last one. The file changes only when you save.

Open it. Edit it. Share it.

  1. 01

    Open

    From the Files app, through AirDrop, or from the share sheet of the app the file is in.

  2. 02

    Edit

    Find the problem on the chart and the map. Apply an edit. Check the numbers. Undo if you change your mind.

  3. 03

    Share

    Save it to Files as a new file or replace the original. Use the share sheet. Or upload it to Strava.

Your file stays on your iPhone or iPad until you share it.

FitFitr needs no account. Analytics and crash reports are off until you switch them on, and they never contain file contents, file names, places or times.
